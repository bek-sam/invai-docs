# Put InvAI's agents on rails, not autopilot

InvAI can become a platform where LLM agents run most of a DTF shop's day, and it should get there by building on code it already has, not by bolting on a chatbot or a new framework. The backend already contains a working, bounded tool-calling agent loop (`runAssistant`), metered and PII-scrubbed model calls, about 45 typed outbox events and about 65 idempotent BullMQ jobs. Those pieces are most of an agent runtime. What is missing is narrow and buildable: agents that can propose write actions, an "agent" identity inside the tenant and RLS model, a policy engine that sorts every action into **auto / notify / approve**, retrieval over shop knowledge in the unused pgvector extension, and trajectory evals. The research supports a roster of about ten event-driven specialist agents (order intake, production, shipping, claims, purchasing, finance, listings, customer service, compliance, and a chief-of-staff supervisor). They react to outbox events, write proposals into one approval inbox, and earn more autonomy only by passing evals. The evidence is clear on two points. Today's agents fail often enough that money-moving and buyer-facing actions must start behind a human gate: GPT-4o-class models solved only about 61% of τ-retail tasks, and fewer than 25% consistently across 8 tries. And no competitor yet links multi-marketplace orders, a scan-verified production floor and shipping in one accountable agent flow, so the opportunity is real. Several parts of the plan collide with accepted decisions: the assistant is "read-only" by scope, "buyer message drafts" are cut, nothing may write prices, listings, ads or POs, and AI design generation is cut (decision 0006). Those are listed below as owner-inbox items with the new evidence, not quietly overridden. The recommended order is seven agent waves of at most five tasks each, starting with paperwork and a foundation wave and ending with eval-gated autonomy promotion.

## A DTF shop loses money on waste and response clocks, not on volume

The plan needs to know where a shop's time and money actually go, so it can aim agents at the expensive parts. A DTF shop's work falls into five labor pools, and agents can help a lot with some and barely at all with others. Design, trend research and listing work takes **10–15 hours a week while a shop is starting and 3–5 hours a week once it settles** ([GoPathToMillions](https://www.gopathtomillions.com/2025/07/print-on-demand-time-management.html)). Some sellers on Etsy's forum describe spending "5 to 10 hours every day" on promotion and listing ([Etsy Community](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Etsy-Success/How-Many-Hours-do-you-Spend-on-Etsy/m-p/26917273)). Laying out a gang sheet (the big film sheet that packs many designs) takes **20–45 minutes by hand versus 2–3 minutes with gang-sheet software**, about a 10x gap ([DTF Gang Sheet App](https://dtfgangsheetapp.com/blog/dtf-print-shop-margins-pricing-strategy); [Cheetah DTF](https://www.cheetahdtf.com/blogs/dtf-transfers/manual-gang-sheets-vs-automatic-gang-sheet-builder-a-complete-cost-quality-comparison)). InvAI's imaging service already closes that gap with deterministic code. Pressing is done by separate hourly workers paid **about $13–$23 an hour** ([ZipRecruiter](https://www.ziprecruiter.com/Jobs/Dtf-Printing)). An LLM cannot press a shirt; it can only make sure the right shirt reaches the press.

The money is in materials and waste. Ink, film and powder are **40–60% of DTF production cost**, about $0.70–$1.20 a print ([Subli-Star](https://www.subli-star.com/is-dtf-printing-expensive-how-to-calculate-it/); [MTU Tech](https://www.mtutech.com/Blog-DTF-Printer/DTF-Printing-Cost-Analysis-Breaking-Down-Price-Per-Print-840.html)). Shops budget **5% for reprints with a manual workflow versus 1.5% when automated**, and some write off 10–15% of supplies early on ([ColDesi](https://coldesi.com/dtf-printing/dtf-print-cost-roi-calculator-free/); [BestPriceDTF](https://bestpricedtf.com/blogs/news/the-hidden-cost-of-control-why-in-house-dtf-is-costing-screen-printers-money)). Nobody has measured a real DTF misprint rate; these are planning allowances. One common cause of a misprint is in a shop's data before it is in its press. Several Etsy threads report buyer personalization arriving **cut off, shortened to the first word, or missing** from the seller's order view ([Etsy Community](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Technical-Issues/Personalization-bug-on-customer-orders/td-p/149097409); [Etsy Community](https://community.etsy.com/t5/Technical-Issues/Personalization-cut-off-on-orders/m-p/139410787)). An agent that reads each order before the sheet is built and holds a suspect one stops waste at its cheapest point.

The second pressure is the marketplace scorecard, and response-time clocks drive it more than order volume does. Amazon suspends accounts whose **order defect rate goes over 1%**. In Q1 2025, **14% of seller accounts faced suspension**, and ODR was the top cause ([SaraSanalytics](https://www.sarasanalytics.com/blog/amazon-order-defect-rate); [Emplicit](https://emplicit.co/amazon-policy-appeals-best-practices/)). TikTok Shop starts order-volume limits once late dispatch reaches 10% and expects seller-fault cancellations at or below 2.5% ([Cahoot](https://www.cahoot.ai/tiktok-shop-fulfillment-requirements/)). Walmart counts lateness at delivery, not at ship time, with an on-time-delivery floor above 90% ([Feedvisor](https://feedvisor.com/university/walmart-seller-performance-standards/)). Etsy's Star Seller badge requires answering **95% of first messages within 24 hours** ([Listybox](https://listybox.com/blog/etsy-customer-service-star-seller-response-time-guide/)). A small seller's actual message load is modest, "an hour or two" a day ([EcomBalance](https://ecombalance.com/amazon-seller-messages/)). So the job is not to handle huge message volume. It is to make sure no clock is missed at night, on weekends or on holidays. That is work an always-on, event-driven agent does well.

Some decisions are less "judgment" than they look. Refund-versus-reprint follows a semi-standard decision tree: a shop's defect gets a free reprint, shipping damage goes to a carrier claim, and a buyer's mistake is denied or paid. Shops typically decide within 7 business days ([PulseMerch](https://pulsemerch.com/how-to-handle-returns/); [Radiant Printing](https://help.radiantprinting.com/en/articles/1970617-reprint-refund-policy)). That is a classification problem an agent can do first-pass. Deciding what to reorder and which designs to list has no codified rule anywhere in the sources, so an agent there would be inventing decision logic, not automating an existing rule. Apparel demand forecasts commonly miss by **20–35% (WAPE) at category level, and by more at size level** ([Verve AI](https://www.getverveai.com/blog/apparel-demand-forecasting)). That argues for proposals a human approves, not autonomous purchasing.

## Nobody runs the whole loop yet, and the failures show why caution pays

The competitive field is busy but shallow. Print-on-demand platforms ship generative content tools. MyDesigns has "Dream AI" for images and "Vision AI" that writes titles and tags, but its workflow stops at "send order to a DTF supplier" ([MyDesigns](https://mydesigns.io/blog/ai-design-tools-for-print-on-demand/)). ShipStation is closest to real agentic operations: its March 2026 "Intelligence" layer turns plain-language prompts into automation rules ([ShipStation](https://www.shipstation.com/press/shipstation-intelligence-announcement/)), but it covers shipping only. Amazon's Seller Assistant can now "reason, plan and take actions **with a seller's approval**" on inventory and account health ([PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/amazon/2025/amazon-expands-seller-assistant-with-agentic-ai-to-become-always-on-partner); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/17/amazon-ai-agent-sellers.html)), and Walmart has Marty for sellers and advertisers ([Pacvue](https://pacvue.com/blog/meet-walmarts-ai-assistants-marty-and-sparky/)). Each of these covers one marketplace and no physical production. No vendor found connects orders from five marketplaces, a scan-checked press floor and labels in one auditable agent flow. That is InvAI's product, and nobody appears to be closing the gap. The note is uncertain about Pythias and STAHLS' Fulfill Engine, which the `competitive-watch` playbook should re-check. The biggest platform builder made the same choice this plan makes: Amazon's agent acts *with approval*.

The documented failures share one root cause. Air Canada's chatbot made up a bereavement-refund rule. A tribunal held the airline liable for "negligent misrepresentation" and rejected the argument that the bot was "a separate legal entity" ([AIBusiness](https://aibusiness.com/nlp/air-canada-held-responsible-for-chatbot-s-hallucinations-); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/marisagarcia/2024/02/19/what-air-canada-lost-in-remarkable-lying-ai-chatbot-case/)). Cursor's support bot "Sam" invented a "one device per subscription" policy, and paying users canceled ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/04/18/cursor_ai_support_bot_lies/)). In both cases an agent was allowed to *state policy it could not cite*. Support automation is real but modest. Gorgias reports a **45% median** of AI-touched tickets resolved end to end, and 65% for the top quarter of brands ([Lorikeet](https://www.lorikeetcx.ai/articles/resolution-rate-ai-customer-support-benchmarks-2026)). Intercom and Zendesk claim 76–80% or more, but they count "resolved" differently ([getmacha](https://www.getmacha.com/blog/ai-customer-service-resolution-rate-statistics)). On general agent reliability, Sierra's τ-bench found function-calling agents succeed on **fewer than 50% of tasks, and on fewer than 25% consistently over 8 tries (pass^8)** ([Sierra](https://sierra.ai/uk/blog/tau-bench-shaping-development-evaluation-agents)). Pass^k means "succeeds on all k repeated attempts." It is the number that matters for an agent left alone overnight. Newer models do better on the easier retail domain ([arXiv 2602.16666](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2602.16666)), but the lesson holds: autonomy must be earned per action with evals, not granted per agent.

Marketplace rules fix the outer boundary. Amazon lets sellers contact buyers **only to complete orders or answer support questions**, never for marketing ([Seller Labs](https://www.sellerlabs.com/knowledge-base/buyer-seller-messaging-rules-what-you-can-and-cant-send/)). A secondary source says Amazon has run every outgoing message through an AI content filter since March 2026 ([Seller Labs](https://www.sellerlabs.com/blog/buyer-seller-messaging-amazon-2026/)); this should be checked against Amazon's own docs. TikTok Shop does not count unlabeled automated replies toward its response SLA ([TikTok Seller University](https://seller-us.tiktok.com/university/essay?knowledge_id=7359788334565162)), and it bans scraping or "simulating human behavior" ([almcorp](https://almcorp.com/blog/tiktok-shop-ai-seller-tools-chatbot-live-stream-automation/)). Etsy requires AI disclosure in the listing description ([growtsy](https://www.growtsy.com/etsy-ai-policy)). InvAI's own research rule is stricter still: "our shipping module and mailer must never email Etsy buyers" ([research 10:130](../research/10-marketplace-engineering-rules.md)). Every agent below works inside these lines: official APIs only, nothing promotional, AI labels where a channel requires them, and no Etsy buyer email, ever.

## InvAI already has most of an agent runtime

The owner's course taught the parts of an agent: a model, tools, a loop, memory, guardrails and evals. InvAI has working versions of most of them. `runAssistant` in [gateway.ts:270](../../invai-backend/src/ai/gateway.ts) is a real multi-round tool-calling loop for Anthropic and OpenAI. It stops at `ASSISTANT_MAX_ITERATIONS`, a test proves it stops even when the model keeps asking for tools ([openai-provider.test.ts:473](../../invai-backend/src/ai/openai-provider.test.ts)), it re-checks spend and credits after every round (`afterRound`, gateway.ts:312), and it scrubs every tool result before it goes back to the model (`isolateToolResults`, `scrubAssistantRun`, gateway.ts:193, 246). In smolagents terms it is a `ToolCallingAgent` written in TypeScript. About 19 tools hang off it in [assistant-tools.ts](../../invai-backend/src/modules/ai/assistant-tools.ts), from `get_profit` to `simulate_price`. Around it sit the safety pieces. Untrusted text only enters prompts inside `<data>` blocks that refuse forged tags ([prompts/index.ts:31](../../invai-backend/src/ai/prompts/index.ts)). `stripPiiDeep` runs before every provider call, under decision 0007's "no PII goes to the AI provider". Each tenant has a monthly credit ledger ([credits.ts](../../invai-backend/src/ai/credits.ts)). Daily platform and per-tenant spend circuit breakers live in Valkey ([breaker.ts](../../invai-backend/src/ai/breaker.ts)). Sample workspaces are forced onto the mock provider (gateway.ts:50). There is an eval harness with one set per route (`invai-backend/evals/run.ts`).

The trigger surface is also built. Every state change commits together with its outbox event, in the same transaction ([architecture-as-built.md:39](../build/architecture-as-built.md)). The outbox is a table of "this happened" records that a relay hands to queues. The events, defined in [events.ts](../../invai-contracts/src/events.ts), cover the whole lifecycle: `order.imported`, `item.needs_mapping`, `artwork.flagged`, `sheet.build_failed`, `item.qc_failed`, `reprint.requested`, `stock.low`, `shipment.status_changed`, `tracking.push_failed`, `connection.sync_failed`, `plan.limit_reached` and more. The relay already fans out to BullMQ queues named `sync`, `render`, `ship`, `ai` and `reports`. Every oRPC procedure declares its permission in metadata (`proc("orders.manage", …)`), and middleware checks it centrally ([architecture-as-built.md:14](../build/architecture-as-built.md)). That means each procedure is close to a typed, permission-checked tool definition already. The risky side effects are already safe to retry. The label buy was rebuilt to be crash-safe after a double-charge bug (backlog B-11). Tenant POs can never fall back to InvAI's own S&S keys (B-55). Production refuses to run side effects on mocks (B-50). An agent that calls `shipping.buy` twice gets one label.

The gaps are specific, and the plan is built around them. First, **no tool can write**. `AssistantTool` is defined as "read-only, company-scoped" ([providers/types.ts:27](../../invai-backend/src/ai/providers/types.ts)). Second, **there is no agent identity**. Roles stop at owner through vendor ([roles.ts:7](../../invai-contracts/src/roles.ts)), `AuthMode` is `user | floor | station | public` ([_base.ts:13](../../invai-contracts/src/contract/_base.ts)), and the only non-human path is `withSystem`, which bypasses RLS ([client.ts:86](../../invai-backend/src/db/client.ts)). Third, **there is no "agent proposed X" event or approval record**. Fourth, **pgvector is installed but unused**: no vector columns and no embeddings ([glossary.md:113](../learn/glossary.md)). Fifth, **no rule covers agents triggering each other** in loops. Research 12 covers OWASP's LLM06 "Excessive Agency" for single calls ([research 12:23](../research/12-security-quality-playbook.md)), but not chains of agents.

## What to build: ten event-driven agents, one gate, one memory

A note on durability, meaning a run that survives crashes. BullMQ "treats one job as one function call"; it does not checkpoint steps the way Temporal or Inngest do ([pkgpulse](https://www.pkgpulse.com/guides/best-nodejs-background-job-libraries-2026)). The cheap fix fits InvAI's conventions. Save each turn to Postgres under a stable run ID, and let each job either advance the run and enqueue the next turn or stop. A wait for human approval becomes a `pending_approval` row, and the approval's outbox event re-enqueues the run. Temporal handles multi-day waits with no process kept alive ([Temporal](https://docs.temporal.io/ai)), but the approval row does the same job until real volume says otherwise.

### The agent roster

Each agent is a *spec* rather than a process. A spec has a name, the events that trigger it, a system prompt, an allow-list of tools, a model and effort level, a per-run budget and an eval set. All specs run on one shared `runAgent` runtime in the `ai` queue (or a new `agents` queue).

### Orchestration: events start agents, and a supervisor fills the gaps

There are two layers. **Layer 1 is a deterministic event router**, a plain lookup table from event type to agent spec, which runs inside the outbox relay's fan-out. Most work never needs an LLM to decide *who* handles it, because the event type already says so. That follows Anthropic's advice to "use the simplest pattern that passes evaluation" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents)). **Layer 2 is the Chief of Staff**, an LLM supervisor that runs on a schedule and on unrouted or escalated events. It reads the 1,000–2,000-token summaries that specialist runs return (Anthropic's context-engineering pattern, [Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/effective-context-engineering-for-ai-agents)) and writes the owner's morning plan. When it hands work to a specialist it uses the orchestrator-worker rule: an explicit objective, an output schema and tool boundaries for each subtask ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/multi-agent-research-system)).

Agents never talk to each other directly. They coordinate through state changes and events, the same way InvAI's human roles do. That makes runaway chains (OWASP ASI08 "Cascading Failures", [OWASP](https://genai.owasp.org/2025/12/09/owasp-genai-security-project-releases-top-10-risks-and-mitigations-for-agentic-ai-security/)) something the system can enforce rules on. Every outbox event an agent action causes carries a `causation_id` and a `depth`. The router refuses to wake an agent on an event its own action caused, caps depth at 3, and caps runs per tenant per hour.

### Identity: an agent is a tenant principal, not a superuser

The inventory note offers two options, impersonating a user or running as a system job. The recommendation combines them. Add an **`agent` role** to [roles.ts](../../invai-contracts/src/roles.ts) with a permission set made of all `*.read` permissions plus new `*.propose` permissions, and no `OWNER_ONLY` permission (`billing.manage`, `org.export`, `org.delete`). Agent code runs inside BullMQ jobs and **always calls `withTenant(companyId, …)`, never `withSystem`**. That keeps every query under RLS (row-level security, the Postgres rule that hides other shops' rows), so a prompt-injected agent still cannot see another tenant. Each tool call is checked against `ROLE_PERMISSIONS.agent` *and* against the tenant's `agent_policies` row before it runs, mirroring the procedure-metadata check. The audit log records `actor = agent:<name>:<runId>` and, for executed proposals, `approved_by = <userId | policy:auto>`, building on the `ai_jobs.createdBy` precedent ([gateway.ts:88](../../invai-backend/src/ai/gateway.ts)). No new HTTP `AuthMode` is needed, because agents never call the API over HTTP. The architect should still record this as an ADR (decision 0025).

### Writes become proposals, and a policy engine chooses the gate

This is the core safety mechanism. A write tool never performs the action. It inserts an `agent_actions` row (`company_id`, agent, run, tool, Zod-validated args, idempotency key = hash(run, tool, args), risk class, evidence links, status) and emits `agent.action_proposed`. A **policy engine** then sorts the action into one of three levels from the featbit/mindstudio gate model: **auto** for safe, reversible actions; **notify** for impactful but recoverable ones, where the agent proceeds and a human can undo; and **approve** for irreversible actions, money or publishing, which wait ([featbit](https://www.featbit.co/blogs/human-in-the-loop-gate-for-agents); [mindstudio](https://www.mindstudio.ai/blog/gate-pattern-ai-agents-prepare-not-submit)). The engine decides with fixed rules (risk class, dollar amount against the tenant's threshold, channel rules) and never by asking the model. Approved actions run through an executor job that calls the **same service function a human's click would call**, so idempotency, events and audit come for free.

One more rule comes from the prompt-injection research. Every single-layer defense has been bypassed, so tools must be locked down "in a turn that consumed retrieved content" ([digitalapplied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/prompt-injection-defense-12-layer-framework-2026)). InvAI's version is a **taint flag**. A run that read untrusted text (buyer personalization, order notes, messages, free-text marketplace fields) is marked tainted, and every outward or money action it proposes is raised to *approve*, whatever the policy says. Delimiting with `<data>` blocks is necessary but not sufficient.

| Action class | Example | Level | Why |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read / analyze / summarize | profit, stock, timeline | auto | No side effect |
| Internal, reversible state | hold order, flag item, tag, assign bin | auto | Undo is one click; prevents waste |
| Internal, costly-to-undo | build gang sheet, request reprint | notify | Consumes film/staff time; reversible before printing |
| Money, idempotent, small | buy label ≤ tenant threshold at cheapest valid rate | approve → notify after eval | Idempotent buy exists (B-11); ShipStation-style rules show the pattern |
| Money, larger or irreversible | PO submit, refund, label void, plan change | approve, always | Forecast error 20–35%; owner-only billing |
| Marketplace-visible | listing publish, tracking push correction | approve (publish), notify (tracking retry) | B-46 trademark gate; disclosure rules |
| Buyer-visible | any message | approve, always; never Etsy email | Air Canada liability; research-10 rule |
| Banned | price/ad changes, design generation, promotional messages, scraping | blocked in policy | scope.md fences, decision 0006, marketplace rules |

### Memory and knowledge: retrieval for documents, SQL for facts

The RAG layer should be small and honest about its job. A new tenant table, `knowledge_chunks` (`company_id`, source type, source id, text, `embedding vector(…)`, `tsvector`, `approved_by`), stores a shop's refund and reprint policy, production SOPs, sizing charts, shipping-time promises, InvAI help articles and *closed* support cases. Retrieval follows the 2026 hybrid best practice: run keyword and vector search in parallel, merge them with Reciprocal Rank Fusion, rerank, and keep the top 3–10 chunks ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/hybrid-search-rag-vector-keyword-reranking-2026); [Instaclustr](https://www.instaclustr.com/education/vector-database/pgvector-hybrid-search-benefits-use-cases-and-quick-tutorial/)). Postgres's built-in full-text search can stand in for BM25 at first, which avoids adding an extension like ParadeDB. Embedding is heavy work, so it runs in a job. Embedding calls go through the gateway so they are metered and PII-scrubbed. The rule that prevents another Cursor-style "Sam" incident: **any policy claim an agent makes must cite a `knowledge_chunks` id, and an answer guard rejects drafts with uncited policy statements.** `answer-guard.test.ts` already exists as a pattern to extend. Orders, stock and money are never embedded, because typed tools answer those exactly.

Buyer PII stays out of the model. The Customer Care Drafter sees `{{buyer_first_name}}` and `{{tracking_url}}` placeholders, and the server fills them in only when it shows the draft to a human. Decision 0007 and the Amazon data-protection evidence pack therefore stay intact.

### Cost: per-tenant, per-agent budgets on the existing breaker

The breaker already counts daily spend in cents per platform and per tenant. Add a third counter, `ai:spend:tenant:<id>:agent:<name>:<date>`, plus a per-run token cap, so one runaway agent cannot use up a shop's whole allowance. Decision 0007 keeps Opus as the default until an eval proves a cheaper model is as good. The plan expects Haiku-class models for classification-heavy agents (intake, claims triage) and stronger models for the supervisor and listings, each change made through the `model-upgrade` playbook. Two levers cut cost a lot. Prompt caching bills cached input at about 10% of the normal rate, and the Batch API halves prices for work that can wait up to 24 hours ([devtoollab](https://devtoollab.com/blog/prompt-caching-guide); [pecollective](https://pecollective.com/tools/claude-pricing-guide/)). Nightly finance, compliance and purchasing runs are good batch candidates. Here is an illustrative sum using aggregator prices, which must be checked against the official price page ([benchlm](https://benchlm.ai/anthropic/api-pricing)). An intake check of about 3,000 input and 300 output tokens on a $1/$5-per-million model costs about $0.0045 per order, or about $9 a month for a 2,000-order shop. The 15x token cost of multi-agent fan-out is why the supervisor reads summaries, not transcripts.

### Observability and evals: if it can't be traced, it can't be promoted

Every run should emit OpenTelemetry GenAI spans with the standard names: `invoke_agent` for a run, `chat` for a model call and `execute_tool` for a tool call, all inside a session span and tagged with `company_id` and agent ([OTel GenAI](https://github.com/open-telemetry/semantic-conventions-genai/blob/main/docs/gen-ai/gen-ai-agent-spans.md)). The `add-observability` playbook should adopt those names as they are. Self-hosted Langfuse (MIT licence) is a reasonable trace and eval UI ([qaskills](https://qaskills.sh/blog/braintrust-vs-langfuse)), but choosing it is an owner and SRE decision. Evals come in four layers. **Unit evals** score each agent's single decisions, such as "should this order be held?", against labeled fixtures. **Trajectory evals** replay seeded scenarios on the mock providers (bad personalization, label buy at a ship-by edge case, a stock-low spike) and check both the final state and the tool sequence. **Pass^k** runs each scenario k times and reports the share that passes every time. **Judge calibration** reports the LLM-as-judge's agreement with a few hundred human labels next to every score ([qaskills](https://qaskills.sh/blog/braintrust-vs-langfuse)).

Autonomy follows a ladder: **draft-only → supervised → spot-checked**, with promotion on eval pass rate and automatic demotion when quality drifts ([featbit](https://www.featbit.co/blogs/human-in-the-loop-gate-for-agents)). Concretely, each (agent, action class, tenant) moves up one level only after N approved proposals with an override rate under a set threshold and a passing pass^k score. It drops back automatically when its override rate rises. There is also a per-tenant kill switch. New agents start in **shadow mode** (they propose, but humans never see it), the same pattern the digest uses under OI-8.

## Conclusion

The research changes the question from "which agent framework?" to "which actions has each agent earned?" InvAI's real advantage is not a model or a framework. It is that every business action already exists as a permission-tagged, idempotent, event-emitting procedure. That makes an agent's proposal as auditable as a human's click, and it is what Air Canada and Cursor lacked. The course's ideas still apply, in a different place than first expected. RAG belongs on policies and SOPs, never on orders. Multi-agent belongs on the event bus, not in agents chatting with each other. Code agents belong nowhere near the app process. Memory is three Postgres tables under RLS.

The useful constraint is the owner's existing fences, which turn out to match the evidence: forecasts are too noisy for autonomous POs, AI designs are legally weak, and unlabeled bot replies don't count toward TikTok's response SLA. Only two fences deserve reopening, read-only tools and buyer-message *drafts*, and both reopen in a gated form that keeps a human on every outbound word and every dollar. An "AI-run business" is reachable as a ladder of measured promotions, not a single switch, and that is a story a shop owner can trust.
