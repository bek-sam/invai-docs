## Census fixture: how it was recorded (AC2)
On 2026-09-27 I made one keyless GET against the live, documented endpoint:
`GET https://api.census.gov/data/timeseries/eits/marts?get=...&category_code=44X&data_type_code=SM&seasonally_adj=no&time=from+2016`
It answered an HTML "Missing Key" page (HTTP 200, not JSON) -- Census requires a key even for
this public dataset, contrary to what the variables endpoint (`.../marts/variables.json`, itself
keyless and confirmed live) suggested. Per the card ("if it fails, build the fixture from the
documented response shape and say so"), I built `fixtures/census-marts-448-monthly.json` from the
documented list-of-lists shape (header row + one row per observation, every Census
timeseries/data endpoint uses this shape) with a plausible NAICS-448 (clothing stores) monthly
sales series, December-peaked, ~2%/yr nominal growth -- **not real Census numbers**, and the code
comment in `providers/census.ts` says so. `data_type_code: "SM"` and `category_code: "448"` are
my best documented-shape guess (the values list for those fields wasn't readable without a key);
flagged `[U]` in the file for whoever gets a real key to confirm.

7. **Round 2b: mock demand shapes follow the niche, not a hash.** Built this round, in two passes.
   - **First pass:** `mock.ts` imports `NICHES` from `src/modules/market/niches.ts` (read-only, per
     the round's instruction) and builds `QUERY_PEAK_MONTHS` (query -> its niche's `peakMonths`).
     A query whose niche has `peakMonths` gets `peakMonthMultiplier`: the *last* listed month is
     the primary peak (undamped curve, up to 2.0x baseline), earlier listed months get a damped
     (0.7x) secondary bump -- so "Halloween -> October" (`[9,10]`), "Christmas -> December"
     (`[11,12]`), "Mother's Day -> May" (`[4,5]`) and "back to school -> August" (`[7,8]`) each
     land unambiguously on the named month, and a two-far-peak niche (`teacher`: May *and*
     August) shows both, distinctly. The seasonal base (30-49, not the general 20-79) keeps a
     2.0x peak under 100 so it never clips and ties with a secondary bump. A query with no
     seasonal niche gets a hash-picked `rising` / `falling` / `flat` trend instead.
   - **Second pass (live-run finding, found by actually running the QA acceptance suite, not
     assumed):** the first pass's per-*query* hash for trend direction broke
     `src/modules/market/compute.ts`'s `nicheSeries`, which averages a niche's queries per week --
     independent per-query directions cancelled to flat, and two acceptance tests
     (`market.acceptance.test.ts`'s and `ai/market.acceptance.test.ts`'s "disagreement" suites)
     failed with "no mock niche series is rising or falling" walking the first 24 niches. Fixed by
     hashing on the niche's own key (`trendGroupKey`), not the query, so every query in one
     non-seasonal niche shares one direction and anchor. That alone raised the failure from "0/20
     non-seasonal niches found" to a real signal, but a second, subtler problem remained: T-18-3's
     `fitTrend` deseasonalizes its 26-week OLS window using a `seasonalityIndex` computed from the
     *whole* 3-year requested history (naive month-of-year averaging, no detrending -- confirmed
     by replicating that exact pipeline, not guessed). A trend confined to one contiguous 26-week
     (6-month) block reads as "those 6 months are seasonally high", and dividing by that index
     cancelled most of the signal back out (measured: a raw +20% g4 fell to +10% after
     deseasonalization, under the +15% "rising" bound). Fixed by shortening the window (26 -> 16
     weeks) and raising the rate (+-5%/week -> +-13%/week) with margin, tuned empirically against
     a faithful replica of the real `seasonalityIndex`/`fitTrend` pipeline (not just this file's
     simpler math) until real taxonomy niches came back `rising`/`falling` *after*
     deseasonalization, then verified against the real `mockDemandSeries` and finally against the
     live acceptance suite itself. Test: `mock.test.ts`'s new describe blocks ("seasonal shapes
     follow the real taxonomy niche", "trend ... rise, fall or stay flat, by hash"): the four
     named peak-month cases, a two-peak niche case, `MOCK_TREND_SHAPES`, and niche-average g4
     checks for `police` (rising, ~68% raw) and `firefighter` (falling, ~-55% raw) -- both
     verified to also clear the threshold *after* T-18-3's real deseasonalization, not just on
     this file's own math. Result: pass; acceptance-suite effect below.
   - **Rising/falling lists (documented, deterministic, source `google_trends`, weekly
     granularity, real taxonomy queries whose niche has no seasonal prior):** out of 173 such
     queries across 39 niches, 47 rise (>= +15%/4wk) and 38 fall (<= -15%/4wk) by the simple
     last-vs-4-weeks-ago ratio; the rest are flat. Sample rising: "funny doctor t-shirt", "medical

## Round 3 (OI-16, single fix)
- Change: `endOfIsoWeekIso` in `invai-backend/src/integrations/market/period.ts` anchored ISO
  week 1 on 1 January. ISO 8601 defines week 1 as the week *containing* 4 January, so any year
  whose 1 Jan falls on Fri/Sat/Sun (2020-2023, 2027, 2028) came out one week early. Fixed by
  computing `jan4 = Date.UTC(year, 0, 4)`, the Monday of week 1 as `jan4 - ((dow(jan4)-1))` days,
  then `+ (week-1)*7` days for week N's Monday, `+6` days and `23:59:59.999` for its Sunday end.

  week itself -- not `period.ts`'s Thursday-shift `isoWeek()`), asserting the `asOf` lands back
